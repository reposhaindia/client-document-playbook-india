# How should financial advisors and mutual fund distributors store client KYC documents in India?

**Short answer:** Financial advisors, mutual fund distributors and insurance advisors should keep client KYC (PAN, Aadhaar, bank proof, photographs, FATCA declarations and nominee details) in **one secure system** with a separate folder per client, individual staff logins, access given client by client, and a record of every file opened. Documents should not sit on WhatsApp or personal email. Advisory firms also act as data fiduciaries under the DPDP Act, with main duties applying from 13 May 2027. ## KYC documents advisors typically hold

- PAN
- Aadhaar or other officially valid address proof
- Photograph
- Bank account proof: cancelled cheque or bank statement
- FATCA and CRS declaration
- Nominee details
- Income and net worth details, where required for suitability
- Risk profile questionnaire
- Signed agreements or mandates

Advisors regulated by SEBI, IRDAI or registered with AMFI also have their own record-keeping requirements. Check them alongside this guide.

## Secure storage, step by step

1. **One folder per client.** Never mix family members in one folder; each PAN gets its own.
2. **Collect through a secure link,** not WhatsApp or email.
3. **Give staff access client by client.** A relationship manager sees only their clients.
4. **Use individual logins,** with two-step verification for the owner.
5. **Keep an access log** and review it regularly.
6. **Remove access the day staff leave.**
7. **Set retention periods** that meet your regulator's rules, and delete documents after that.

## Why this matters for advisory firms

- Clients trust advisors with identity, bank and wealth details.
- Staff and relationship managers move between firms.
- Regulators expect records to be maintained properly and kept secure.
- Under the DPDP Act, the firm must protect clients' personal data with reasonable security safeguards.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can advisors store client KYC on Google Drive?**
It can work if every staff member has their own account, access is limited per client and activity is logged, but that takes careful setup. A client document service built for firms does this by default.

**How long should advisors keep KYC records?**
It depends on the regulator and the product. Keep records for the period your regulator requires, then delete them.

**Do family members need separate KYC folders?**
Yes. Separate folders per PAN keep access control and records clean.
