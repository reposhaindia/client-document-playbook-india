# What does the DPDP Act mean for CA firms?

**Short answer:** Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, a CA firm that collects clients' personal data (PAN, Aadhaar, bank statements, salary and family details) acts as a **data fiduciary**. The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025, and the main duties for data fiduciaries apply from **13 May 2027**. Firms should get ready now: collect documents through one secure channel, limit staff access client by client, keep access logs, have a breach plan, and set retention periods. ## Is a CA firm a "data fiduciary" under the DPDP Act?

Yes, in most cases. A data fiduciary is anyone who decides why and how personal data is processed. When a CA firm collects a client's Aadhaar, PAN, bank statements or Form 130 to file a return, the firm decides how that data is stored, who sees it and how long it is kept.

This applies whatever the firm's size. A sole practitioner holding documents for 40 clients has the same basic duties as a large firm.

## Key DPDP dates for CA firms

| Date | What applies |
|---|---|
| 11 August 2023 | DPDP Act enacted |
| 13 November 2025 | DPDP Rules, 2025 notified. Data Protection Board provisions in force |
| 13 November 2026 | Consent manager provisions apply |
| 13 May 2027 | Data fiduciary duties apply: notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting, retention and data principal rights |

## What a CA firm must do under the DPDP Act

### 1. Collect for a clear purpose, with notice

Tell clients what personal data you collect and why, in simple language. The engagement letter or onboarding message is a natural place to do this.

### 2. Protect data with reasonable security safeguards

The Rules expect reasonable security measures. For a CA firm, that means in practice:

- Client documents stored in one controlled system, not scattered across staff phones and inboxes
- Access limited to the staff who work on each client
- Individual logins, with two-step verification for the owner
- A record of who accessed what
- Backups, so documents are not lost

### 3. Report personal data breaches

If client data is exposed, for example through a lost phone full of client documents, the firm must inform the Data Protection Board and the affected clients. The Rules require a detailed report to the Board within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach.

### 4. Keep data only as long as needed

Personal data should be erased once its purpose is over, unless another law requires it to be kept. Tax, GST and audit laws do require some records for set periods, so firms need a retention rule per service.

### 5. Respond to clients' rights

Clients can ask what data the firm holds, ask for corrections and ask for erasure, subject to legal retention duties. The Rules set 90 days to respond.

### 6. Make sure vendors protect data too

If the firm uses a cloud service or software to store client documents, the firm remains responsible. Choose vendors that store data securely and in India where possible.

## The biggest DPDP gap in Indian CA firms: WhatsApp

Most firms collect documents on WhatsApp and email. That makes the security duties very hard to meet:

- Documents sit on staff phones and personal cloud backups.
- There is no access control and no record of who opened what.
- A single lost or changed phone can become a reportable breach.

Moving document collection to secure upload links is the single largest step a firm can take. See [How can a CA firm collect client documents without WhatsApp?](how-to-collect-client-documents-without-whatsapp.md)

## DPDP readiness checklist for CA firms

1. List what personal data you collect, for which service.
2. Add a short privacy notice to your engagement letter or onboarding message.
3. Stop collecting client documents on personal WhatsApp.
4. Keep all client documents in one secure system.
5. Give staff access client by client, with individual logins.
6. Turn on two-step verification for owner accounts.
7. Keep access logs and review them monthly.
8. Write a one-page breach response plan with named responsibilities.
9. Set a retention period per service, and delete what you no longer need.
10. Remove access the day a staff member or article leaves.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the DPDP Act apply to small CA firms?**
Yes. The Act does not exempt firms by size. A small practice holds the same kind of sensitive client data as a large one.

**What is the penalty under the DPDP Act?**
Penalties are set by the Data Protection Board based on the breach. The highest penalty, for failing to take reasonable security safeguards, can go up to ₹250 crore.

**Is a firm "DPDP compliant" just by using a particular software?**
No. Compliance is the firm's duty as data fiduciary. Good tools make it far easier, by giving the firm access control, logs and secure storage, but the firm's own policies and practices matter too.

**Can a CA firm keep client documents after the engagement ends?**
Yes, for as long as tax, GST, company or audit laws require, and as long as the firm needs them to defend its work. After that, they should be deleted.
