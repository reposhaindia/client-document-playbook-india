# How can a CA firm track which staff opened client files?

**Short answer:** A CA firm can only track who opened a client file if its documents sit in a system that **logs every view and download by user**. WhatsApp, email, pen drives and most shared folders keep no such record. The practical fix is to keep client documents in one place, give each staff member their own login, give access client by client, and review the access log regularly. Client document services such as [Reposha](https://reposha.com) record every open and download for each client automatically.

---

## Why CA firms need to know who opened client files

- **Confidentiality is the core of the profession.** Clients trust the firm with Aadhaar, PAN, bank statements, salary details and business accounts.
- **Staff turnover is high.** Articles complete their training and move on. Staff change firms every season.
- **Questions come later.** When a client asks "who saw my documents?", the firm needs a real answer, not a guess.
- **Data protection law.** Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, firms holding client personal data are expected to protect it with reasonable safeguards. Knowing who accessed what is a basic one.

## Why most firms cannot track access today

| Where documents sit | Can you see who opened a file? |
|---|---|
| WhatsApp chats | No |
| Email inboxes | No |
| Pen drives and office PCs | No |
| Shared login on one drive | No, everyone appears as the same user |
| Shared drive with individual logins | Sometimes, often only on paid business plans and hard to read |
| Client document service with access logs | Yes, per user, per file, with date and time |

The biggest single gap is the **shared login**. If the whole office uses one account, no log can tell staff members apart.

## Five steps to track who opens client files

1. **Move client documents into one system.** Tracking is impossible while files are spread across phones, inboxes and drives.
2. **Give every person their own login.** No shared office accounts. One person, one login.
3. **Give access client by client.** Staff and articles should only see the clients they work on. Fewer people with access means a shorter, more useful log.
4. **Turn on two-step verification** for the owner's account at minimum, so a stolen password alone cannot open everything.
5. **Review the log on a schedule.** Glance at it monthly, and always when a staff member leaves or a client raises a concern.

## What a good access log should show

- Which staff member opened or downloaded the file
- Which client and which document
- Date and time
- Whether it was a view or a download

A log is only useful if a partner can read it in a minute. Look for one that can be filtered by client and by staff member.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can Google Drive show who opened a file?**
Some business plans show file activity, but only if each person has their own account, the files were never downloaded and shared elsewhere, and someone checks it. It was built for sharing, so collecting from clients and limiting staff to their own clients still needs work.

**Is it worth tracking access in a small firm with three or four staff?**
Yes. Small firms are hit hardest when one person leaves with client data, and it takes no extra effort once the log is automatic.

**Will staff feel they are being watched?**
Frame it as protecting clients and the staff themselves. If a question ever comes up, the log shows exactly who did and did not access a file.
